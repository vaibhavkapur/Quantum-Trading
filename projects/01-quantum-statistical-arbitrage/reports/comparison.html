Does a quantum-assisted basket selector beat classical methods at equal budget?

Walk-forward comparison on the demo experiment · generated from 2 stored run(s)

No quantum advantage was observed. At an identical budget, simulated QAOA found the optimal basket in 78% of fold-seed runs (mean normalised gap 0.035) versus 100% for CP-SAT (gap 0.000), while taking 1.08 s per solve against 12.4 ms (about 88x slower). The budget-matched random control reaches 91% optimal through the same repair step, so most of QAOA's final quality here comes from classical repair rather than from the circuit.

Limitations

  • Data are synthetic with planted structure; real markets add nonstationarity, borrow constraints and regime change.
  • Instances are small enough for exact enumeration, so a quantum advantage is not expected and none is claimed.
  • The objective is a pairwise surrogate: it cannot see cointegration that only appears in triples.
  • QAOA numbers are noiseless simulation; device noise, connectivity and queueing would only make them worse.
  • Thresholds and weights in the config were not tuned on the test windows, but multiple-testing risk remains if they are iterated.
